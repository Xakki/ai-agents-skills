# Security and operational boundaries

Carry current authorization, privacy and lifecycle rules into every proposed option; a recommendation does not waive them. Distinguish removing an index entry, archive membership, revocation and physical deletion, and require authority for the exact operation and resource scope. Trace dependent history, ownership, retention and cleanup effects before proposing changes. Avoid inventing a universal retention or deletion policy.

For authentication, worker or external-input work, define relevant identity precedence, tenant isolation, trusted ingress, execution and network/filesystem boundaries, secret handling, logs, retention, role/plan gates and both allowed and denied tests. Distinguish execution routing from output classification. For remote URL access, consider redirects, DNS and destination checks at every permitted hop. Keep missing product/security choices in grooming; a technical default is not owner consent.

A residual-risk acceptance must record the actual approved scope and any agreed owner or expiry/review condition; do not turn a canceled optional mitigation into an invented recurring gate. Security requirements that remain independently mandatory are not waived. Treat secret-bearing files as owner-managed sources: do not copy their contents into cards, broad searches, prompts or test artifacts.
