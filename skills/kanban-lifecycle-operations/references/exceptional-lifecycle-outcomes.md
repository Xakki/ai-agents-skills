# Exceptional lifecycle outcomes

## Cancellation and duplicates

Distinguish owner-authorized cancellation, temporary freeze, duplicate retirement and completed implementation. For a cancellation, preserve prior decisions as historical and record the decision and applicable reason; replace active implementation criteria with administrative closure checks, without claiming the work was implemented. Do not invent a return condition. Use only a sanctioned archive action and authorized exact scope; do not manufacture intermediate delivery stages or bypass a native refusal.

When duplicate IDs exist, compare complete records and establish the canonical result and the disposition of unique evidence before any authorized cleanup. Verify the expected single physical record and status. A warning alone does not authorize deleting either copy.

## Already delivered or accepted work

When implementation is already integrated, qualify the actual source, review and acceptance evidence and the board's supported reconciliation route. Record the action-specific approval for the exact target. Do not infer publication or deployment authority from lifecycle completion. Repair an accepted card in place where appropriate; preserve prior receipts and record the correction's own gates instead of replaying transitions for appearance.

## Epic handoff

Check each approved child's canonical current state and actual embedded status fields; do not invent fields. Record the parent snapshot and review/acceptance gates, and verify the parent against its own rules. An intermediate child commit does not complete the epic. Stop at the authorized endpoint and qualify each later action separately.
